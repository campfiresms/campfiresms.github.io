CallMeMCP vs Campfire SMS

An iPhone app your agent can ring, compared with SMS to the phone you already have.

By Campfire SMS · Published October 12, 2026

/call-me, the app at callmemcp.com, lets your AI agent ring your iPhone when it needs a decision. The call screen comes from the /call-me app, and its 10-digit number isn't a real phone number: it exists only inside the app and works over Wi-Fi or mobile data. Campfire SMS sends real text messages to your own phone number, over the carrier network, to any iPhone or Android phone with no app to install. You reply in plain words and the agent reads it. Pick /call-me if you want a ringing call and use an iPhone. Pick Campfire SMS if you want real texts in your normal inbox.

What /call-me (CallMeMCP) does

/call-me is an iPhone app from an independent developer in Norway. Its site, callmemcp.com, opens with "Your AI agent can call you." You install the app, it assigns you a private /call-me number, and you connect your agent to its hosted MCP server at https://callmemcp.com/mcp. When the agent calls, your iPhone rings on the native call screen, so you can answer from the lock screen or with AirPods. The app reads the message aloud (up to 2,000 characters, about two minutes of speech), transcribes your answer, and returns it to the agent as text. See the /call-me homepage.

It isn't call-only. Agents can also send progress texts into the app, each agent session gets its own named thread, and you can type a reply there. Those texts are app notifications, not SMS: the /call-me number works only inside the app, and calls and texts travel over Wi-Fi or mobile data. Per its privacy policy, call audio is transcribed live and not stored, while message text and transcripts are kept until you delete your number.

The radres/call-me repository on GitHub holds the agent-side pieces: a Claude Code plugin, an agent skill, and a callme command-line tool for Codex, scripts, and cron jobs.

A call or a text: which interruption fits

Both products solve the same problem: your agent is working while you're away, and it needs to reach you. The difference is how hard it interrupts you.

  • A call is hard to miss and expects an answer now. It suits a blocking decision, such as "Should I promote this build to production?", when you'd rather be interrupted than find the agent idle an hour later. The /call-me README says its command-line call waits until you answer, by design.
  • A text can wait. It suits status updates, results, and questions you can answer between other things. Your reply sits in the same thread as the agent's question, in the inbox you already check, and you can scroll back through it later.
  • Voice is hands-free. Text is exact. Speaking works while you drive or cook. Typing is better for branch names, file paths, numbers, and short commands like "yes, ship it".

Whichever you pick, tell the agent when to reach you. A short rule works well: "Text me when the build finishes or when you need a decision. Don't text for routine progress." See agent questions you can answer by SMS for examples.

Choose /call-me if…

  • You want a ringing call. A native iPhone call is harder to sleep through or ignore than a notification.
  • You'd rather talk than type. Your spoken answer is transcribed and sent to the agent, and AirPods work.
  • You use an iPhone with iOS 17 or later and don't mind installing an app and starting a subscription.
  • You want to connect by pasting one URL. Its hosted MCP server needs nothing installed on your computer, and its site lists chat apps like ChatGPT and claude.ai alongside coding agents.
  • The briefing is long. A call can read out up to 2,000 characters.

Choose Campfire SMS if…

  • You want the agent you already run (Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Grok Bot, OpenCode, or Grok Build) to text you when it needs an answer or has a result.
  • You use an Android phone, or you don't want another app on your iPhone. Texts arrive in your normal SMS inbox.
  • You want real SMS to your own number, delivered over cell service, rather than app notifications that need Wi-Fi or mobile data.
  • You'd rather answer when it suits you than take a call, and you want a written record of what you told the agent.
  • You use Grok Bot and want a text to start its cloud routine while your PC is off.
  • You'd rather pay per message than start a subscription. The first 40 messages are free, and one-time packs never expire.

Setup is three steps: sign up at campfiresms.com, verify your phone (40 free texts, no card), and run the one-time install command from your account. Then send a test text and reply. See the setup guide for each agent.

Not the open-source CallMe plugin

/call-me is a different project from CallMe, an MIT-licensed plugin that lets Claude Code call you on the phone. That plugin rings your real phone number through a telephony account you bring (Telnyx or Twilio), uses an OpenAI API key for speech-to-text, and uses ngrok to tunnel the provider's webhooks to your computer. You pay those providers directly, per minute.

If you were looking at that plugin because you don't want to manage a telephony account, webhooks, or a tunnel, Campfire SMS removes that work for texts. See Twilio for Codex and Claude Code: DIY vs hosted SMS.

What Campfire SMS does not do

Campfire SMS does not place calls, read messages aloud, or transcribe voice replies. If your phone has no cell service, texts wait until it does.

  • Local agents need a live session. Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, OpenCode, Grok Build, and other local agents need the computer awake and online and the session running. Campfire SMS does not wake a stopped local session. Grok Bot is the exception: an active webhook routine can start it in the cloud when you text.
  • Replies don't grant new permissions. A text can steer the task, but the agent keeps the permissions it already had. Native approvals stay in the agent's own interface.
  • SMS isn't end-to-end encrypted. Don't text secrets, and keep sensitive detail on the computer.

Common questions

Is CallMeMCP the same as /call-me?

Yes. callmemcp.com is the website for /call-me, an iPhone app that lets AI agents call you. Its hosted MCP server is at https://callmemcp.com/mcp. It is a different project from the open-source CallMe plugin for Claude Code.

Is the /call-me number a real phone number?

No. Its FAQ says the number exists only inside the /call-me app, and calls travel over Wi-Fi or mobile data with no SIM or carrier involved. Campfire SMS sends real text messages to your own mobile number over the carrier network.

Does Campfire SMS call my phone?

No. Campfire SMS sends and receives text messages only. It does not place calls or read messages aloud. If you want your agent to ring you and hear a spoken answer, /call-me is built for that.

Does /call-me work on Android?

As of October 2026, the /call-me FAQ says there is no Android version right now and the app needs an iPhone with iOS 17 or later. Campfire SMS needs no app, so replies work from the normal messages app on an iPhone or an Android phone.

Do I need an app or a webhook for Campfire SMS?

No. Sign up, verify your phone, and run the one-time install command. A local agent does need its computer awake and its session running.

What does /call-me cost?

/call-me is a free download, and a subscription is required. As of October 2026, its App Store listing shows /call-me Weekly at $4.99, with a 3-day free trial for new subscribers, and /call-me Yearly at $24.99. It publishes no call or message limits. Check the App Store for current pricing.
